# White-line receive audio interface for the EasyCAP (MS2106) — draft 1

Status: **proposed design, not built or tested** (2026-10-27). It connects the ML-20HD white
(audio/control) wire to the EasyCAP white (left) RCA audio input, receive direction only.
It does not transmit audio, drive unlock, or connect to the red wire.

## Design inputs (measured)

From the OSC v2 recordings (buffered front end: rec5–rec8, blackboard recs1; rec2–rec4 were
loaded by the old 47 kΩ/10 kΩ divider and are used only for timing):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| White DC, standby / talking | 4.56–4.6 V; slow drifts to about 5.2 V; about 6.7 V for seconds after unlock |
| Unlock | 4.6 → 13.3 V (max 13.52 V), 2.01 s; rise faster than one 0.1 ms sample (≥45 V/ms measured) |
| Unlock release | falls to about 6.7 V within 1–2 ms (4–14 V/ms) |
| Incoming-call pulse | 4.6 → 0.00 V, 110–220 ms, fall 2–6 ms |
| Speech, AC (20 ms moving-mean removed) | 16–40 mV RMS; 99.9th percentile ±85–340 mV |
| Isolated transients (state switching) | up to about ±1.5 V |
| Source model | about 47 kΩ combined DC model only; audio impedance unmeasured |

EasyCAP white input (Windows, current capture level): full scale about 2.94 V peak
(about 2.1 V RMS), 0.09 mV/count, idle noise about 0.1 mV RMS. The stream is 48 kHz stereo
mislabelled as 96 kHz mono; white = even samples (left).

U1 = MCP6022 (rail-to-rail, 5.5 V absolute maximum supply), same part and pinout as the
scope buffer. Another single-supply rail-to-rail op-amp rated for 5 V also works
(e.g. MCP6002, TLV9062, OPA2340); check its pinout.

## How it works and why these values

- **No DC load on the line.** C1 blocks the 4.6 V standby level, so the line sees
  R1 + R2 ≈ 1.1 MΩ for audio and nothing at DC. This is similar to the scope buffer's
  1.254 MΩ, which did not disturb the line; the old 47 kΩ/10 kΩ divider did.
- **High-pass 14 Hz** (C1 with R1 + R2). Speech (≥150 Hz) passes unchanged, and the node
  recovers quickly after control steps: time constant 11 ms, back to normal within about
  60 ms after the 13 V unlock edge, the 0 V call pulse, or the unlock release.
- **Protection.** D1/D2 hold node X between about −0.5 V and VCC + 0.5 V. Worst case
  (+8.9 V unlock step) the clamp current is (8.9 − 2.9) V / 100 kΩ ≈ 60 µA, decaying with
  τ = R1·C1 = 1 ms; R1 limits it, and C4 easily absorbs it. R3 limits current into the
  op-amp's internal input diodes (MCP6022 inputs are rated only VSS − 0.3 V to VDD + 0.3 V).
- **Unity-gain buffer.** The EasyCAP's input impedance is unknown; the op-amp drives it
  from a low impedance, so the line never sees it. Output can never exceed 0…VCC, i.e.
  ±2.4 V around VMID after C2, below the EasyCAP's 2.94 V full scale. Control steps
  appear as short clicks that the Pi software mutes, as the monitor does.
- **Level.** R1 and R2 divide the input to 91 % (−0.8 dB); C1's impedance (16 kΩ at
  1 kHz, 53 kΩ at 300 Hz) is negligible against 1.1 MΩ. Speech 99.9th percentile
  ±0.31 V at node X ≈ −19.5 dBFS; RMS about −38 dBFS; 50 dB
  above the EasyCAP floor. Apply gain digitally on the Pi. If a hardware gain of 2–3 is
  wanted later, it can be added to U1A's feedback (transients then clip at the rails, harmlessly).
- **Output.** C2 removes the 2.4 V bias; R5 defines the output at 0 V DC and discharges
  C2 when unplugged; R4 isolates cable capacitance from the op-amp. Corner well below 10 Hz.
- **Supply filter.** R8/C4 (corner 16 Hz) keeps Pi/USB 5 V noise out of VCC and VMID.
  The phone-charger test showed supply noise couples straight into this input at
  about −50 dBFS, so do not omit it. R8 drops about 0.1 V (MCP6022 ≈ 1 mA).

## Bench test procedure (before the intercom)

Use the STM32 scope, the DMM, the phone (charger unplugged) and the STM32 board's 5 V/3.3 V.

1. **Power only, no input, EasyCAP unplugged.** DMM: VCC ≈ 4.8–4.9 V; VMID, node X and
   U1 pin 1 ≈ VCC/2; output (after C2) ≈ 0 V after a few seconds.
2. **Gain check.** Phone 1 kHz tone at the same volume as calibration, phone tip → WHITE
   input, phone sleeve → GND; EasyCAP plugged in. Recording should show about 660 counts
   peak: R1/R2 pass 1 MΩ / 1.1 MΩ = 91 % (the direct connection gave 729). No added noise.
3. **Step test (protection).** Scope CH on U1 pin 1. Touch the WHITE input to 5 V, then
   to GND, then to 3.3 V. Expect a short excursion that stays within 0…VCC and returns to
   VCC/2 within about 0.1 s. If a 12–14 V supply is available, repeat with it (it simulates
   unlock); U1 pin 1 must still stay within 0…VCC.
4. Only then connect the intercom (next section).

## First intercom connection

Keep the Slinex monitor connected as the reference. Connect panel **black → GND** and
**white → input**; **never connect red**. Keep the scope buffer on white in parallel so the
scope and the EasyCAP record the same events. Exercise viewing, Accept/talk, unlock and an
incoming call. Check: monitor behaviour unchanged; EasyCAP not clipping except at control
steps; recovery within about 0.1 s; hum or noise level (ground loop between the Pi or PC
supply and the intercom supply). If hum appears, an audio isolation transformer after C2
is the next option.

## Open points

- EasyCAP input impedance and DC bias at its RCA centre pin (measure with a DMM, EasyCAP
  powered, nothing connected) — affects only the output RC.
- Pi capture level and full scale must be re-measured on the Pi (ALSA).
- Transmit (microphone onto white) and unlock drivers are separate, undesigned circuits.
